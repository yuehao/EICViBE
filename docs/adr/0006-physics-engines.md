# ADR 0006: Physics engines behind one contract

Status: proposed (2026-10-08). Scope: `eicvibe.engines`. Depends on ADR 0001 (lattice, conventions) and ADR 0003 (settings as `(occurrence, parameter)` values, the engine worker of the virtual accelerator).

## Decisions

1. **One contract for every engine.** Requests and results (`eicvibe.engines.contracts`) are plain data in the lattice's conventions: MAD-X/PALS coordinates `x, px, y, py, zeta, delta`, phase advances in turns, values at the exit of every occurrence, keyed by occurrence id. No engine object crosses the contract, so a session can later run in a worker process (ADR 0003) without changing callers.
2. **Four axes say what an engine can do.** *Fidelity* (`linear`, `fast`, `standard`, `precise`): how exactly elements are integrated. *Effects* (longitudinal motion, radiation damping and excitation, space charge, wakes, beam-beam, IBS, spin, apertures): physics beyond the transverse reference dynamics. *Time scale* (equilibrium, single pass, turns, cycle). *Computation* (optics, orbit, tracking). Each engine declares `Capabilities`: native, approximate or no support, with a note on every gap, and effects a computation lacks although the engine has them (TrackPad's optics are 4D). A request an engine cannot meet is refused with the reasons (`UnsupportedRequest`); an engine never drops an effect silently, and accepts an approximation only when the request allows it.
3. **The longitudinal effect means the same in every engine.** With it, the RF acts: 6D optics, a ring's closed orbit at the momentum the RF sets, 6D tracking. Without it, every computation is transverse: cavities are drifts and the momentum stays as given. (MAD-X's TWISS instead searches a 6D closed orbit whenever cavities are on; on the test ring a corrector's orbit is a fifth smaller that way.)
4. **A session compiles once and takes settings in place.** `Engine.open(lattice, branch, options, values)` builds the engine's model; `Session.update(values)` changes the parameters settings and errors change (`model.SETTINGS`: multipole strengths and rolls, RF voltage and phase, solenoid strength, displacement, roll, pitch and yaw; the last two since 2026-10-13, ADR 0011), keyed as the power circuits produce them. A change that would alter the model's structure is refused; it needs a new session.
5. **One reading of the lattice.** `eicvibe.engines.convert` reads what parameters mean (integrated strengths, a bend's field and edges, displacement, reference particle, RF frequency from a harmonic, a patch's frame) for every engine. Adapters differ only in how they build elements.
6. **What spans branches or time sits above the engines.** `machine_optics` computes every branch: rings from their periodic solution, lines from their own initial conditions or from the optics at the fork leading to their start (also a fork landing on a septum's entrance). `quasi_static` follows a `Program` of settings through a cycle by snapshots, which engines declare as an approximate `cycle` time scale.
7. **Engines load lazily and are chosen by capability.** `engine(name)` imports an adapter on first use; `select` returns the first installed engine, in a preferred order, that can do a request.
8. **A server runs each engine in a worker process** (`eicvibe.engines.worker.EngineWorker`). The worker loads the engine once and keeps it loaded (Xsuite's kernels; Julia and TrackPad's compiled code, about 20 s to start); Julia gets the main thread of its own process, and a crash ends the worker, not the server. Requests and results cross as JSON. `machine_optics(..., notes=...)` reports for every branch how its optics started (periodic solution, initial conditions, the fork it starts from) or why it has none, and a branch that fails no longer stops the others. The viewer's optics (ADR 0004) use it; the virtual accelerator will.
9. **Optics inside elements, where an engine can sample them without changing its results.** `OpticsRequest(inside=True)` adds an `OpticsProfile` to the result: points inside elements, each segment tagged with its occurrence. TrackPad samples magnets at its integrator steps (`twiss(...; sample_integrator_steps=true)`: one piece per step, each integrated in one step), so the values at element exits are unchanged (to 1e-10). Its `max_step` would split magnets finer than their steps, which refines the integration and moves results (the BAR's vertical tune by 6e-5 at the standard fidelity), so it is not used. With coupling, uncoupled phase advances summed piece by piece depend on the cut, so a ring's tunes take the fractional part from the one-turn map and only the integer from the summed phase.
10. **Bunches are tracked as BPMs see them.** `Session.track_beam(coordinates, turns, observe, options)` tracks a bunch for a chunk of turns and returns a `BeamTrack`: numpy arrays of the centroid and particle count at every observation point on every turn, and the final coordinates to go on from, instead of every particle at every point. Xsuite averages with its beam statistics monitors, TrackPad in the Julia helper. With `start` and `end` (occurrence ids) it tracks one pass through that part of the branch, as a route's segment needs. With `ramps` (`Ramp`: settings' start and end values and a fraction per turn of the call) settings move turn by turn inside the call, as magnets follow their supplies, and the session is left where they are. Both engines use that the elements' inputs are linear in the settings (checked at each ramp's middle): TrackPad's Julia helper remakes, before a turn, only the elements whose fraction moved, from rows `base + Σ fraction·delta`; Xsuite sets the moving fields straight on the line's elements and tracks between the turns where they move (an element that is not linear is made anew, or refused by TrackPad). A ramped call equals setting each turn's values and tracking that turn: BPM readings to 1e-15 in both engines. The virtual rings and routes of ADR 0007 run on it, through `EngineWorker.open_session`, sessions kept open in the worker (one per branch of a route).
11. **Every RF phase reference converts.** `RFP.zero_phase` gives phi0 in an energy gain of sin(phase + phi0): 0 below transition (MAD-X's 2 pi LAG), pi/2 for `ACCELERATING` (the PALS default: phase 0 is the crest) and pi above transition.
12. **Matching is least squares over sessions.** `match_lines` (`eicvibe.engines.match`) finds values of chosen settings (quadrupole strengths) that bring the optics where each of several stretches of line ends to its targets (beta, alpha, dispersion and its slope), from the optics where it starts, with any engine: a session per branch, `Session.update` and `OpticsRequest(start, end)` per step, SciPy's bounded least squares. Stretches are computed in order and may depend on each other (`At`): one may start from another's optics at a point (a line leaving a fork) or have to end with another's (two paths that must meet with the same beam). `match_line` is the case of one stretch. Misses are measured against a physical scale (1 % of beta, 0.01 in alpha, 0.1 mm of dispersion, 10 µrad of its slope) and the match succeeds when each is within it. Strengths stay within bounds, with `keep_signs` on their own side of zero (a unipolar supply keeps its polarity), beta inside each stretch below its limit, and as close to where they started as the targets allow (a small pull toward the start); two random starts follow if the first ends short. It reports `Bmag` (1 when matched) before and after for each stretch, the largest beta, and the evaluations; a start that already meets the targets is kept as it is. The result is a set of values, which callers write into the lattice's design (`set_design`, `with_design`; ADR 0005) or as an operating mode.

13. **Every engine is an `EngineAdapter`, and passes one conformance suite** (2026-10-12). `Engine` and `Session` (Protocols) describe the contract, for callers and for proxies such as the worker's `RemoteSession`; an adapter implements it by subclassing `EngineAdapter` and `EngineSession` (`eicvibe.engines.adapter`), whose public methods make the contract's checks the same way for every engine before calling the engine's own (`_open`, `_update`, `_optics`, `_orbit`, `_track`, `_track_beam`): installed or not, the capabilities a request needs (time scale included), which parameters change in place and on which branch (updates and ramps alike), a part of a branch from `start` to `end`, a line's optics needing initial conditions, a line passed once and without ramps, observation points inside the part tracked. Engines therefore refuse the same requests for the same reasons, and no adapter can skip a check; before, each adapter repeated them (the setting checks three times over). `engine(name)` refuses an adapter that is not an `EngineAdapter`, and one that leaves a computation out cannot be made. What the engines compute is held to `tests/engines/test_conformance.py`, run for every registered engine against references that are not an engine's: a ring and a line of thick-quadrupole FODO cells whose transfer matrices give the optics exactly (tunes, beta, alpha and phase advance to 1e-7; no dispersion), the closed orbit of a kick against linear optics (1e-4 of its size), the corrector signs (a positive `Kn0L` deflects toward -x, a positive `Ks0L` toward +y), an update in place equal to a session opened with its values (1e-12), the tune of turn-by-turn tracking, a bunch's centroids equal to its particles' mean, a ramp leaving its settings at its end, a quadrupole offset, pitched and yawed about its centre moving the beam as the exact matrices say (2026-10-13, ADR 0011), and the same refusals. Xsuite and TrackPad pass it (2026-10-12). Comparisons of engines with each other and with MAD-X stay in their own tests.

## Adding an engine

1. An adapter module with an `EngineAdapter` (`name`, `unavailable`, `available`, `capabilities` stating what it can and cannot do, `_open`) and an `EngineSession` (the six engine methods; `checked` on any values a tracking call applies), registered in `ENGINES`.
2. `tests/engines/test_conformance.py` passes for it; then its own tests: against MAD-X or another engine on the EIN's rings, at each fidelity it declares.
3. Its findings and validation recorded here.

## The first two engines

| | Xsuite (`xsuite.py`) | TrackPad (`trackpad.py`, `trackpad.jl`) |
|---|---|---|
| Runs | in process (xtrack, prebuilt kernels) | Julia in process through juliacall; `src/eicvibe/juliapkg.json` declares TrackPad and PolySeries |
| Fidelity | fast: linear bend edges, bends in ten Yoshida kicks, expanded drifts; standard: full edges (as MAD-X's thick bends); precise: exact bend body and drifts | fast: AT-style bend, 2 integrator steps; standard: Forest's exact bend, 10 steps; precise: 40 steps. Drifts always exact |
| Effects | longitudinal, radiation damping and excitation | longitudinal (orbit and tracking; optics are 4D) |
| Elements | multipoles to order 10 | multipoles to octupole; no thin bends or solenoids |

TrackPad's elements follow the Accelerator Toolbox: `polynom_b[n+1] = Kn_n/n!`, a bend's `polynom_b[1]` is its field beyond the curvature, displacement and roll are the entrance and exit maps. Coordinates convert as `z = zeta/beta0`, `dE = (E - E0)/(P0 c)`. Julia starts from the real binary behind the juliaup launcher, which juliacall cannot load.

## Validation (2026-10-08)

- Xsuite against Xsuite's own MAD-X import: equal to 1e-15 element by element, tunes to 1e-10 on the test ring. Against MAD-X: the EIN BAR and RCS tunes to 1e-6, betas to 1e-5.
- TrackPad against Xsuite, both precise: thin kicks, patches, translations and RF equal to round-off; thick magnets within 1e-3 of their effect; ring tunes to 1e-7 and betas to 2e-7 (BAR, RCS); every EIN branch's betas to 1e-6 (the RTE, 4 cm off axis after its extraction patch, to 2e-3). TrackPad's 6D closed orbit with a corrector equals MAD-X's to 1e-8, and the BAR's chromaticity MAD-X's to 5e-4.

## Validation (2026-10-11): the EIN matched, with the BAR transparent

The BAR and the RCS each had their periodic optics, but neither route brought the beam into the RCS with them (Bmag 3.1 and 1.4 on the direct route with the design files' strengths, which were matched to an earlier RCS), and the BAR route needed the designers' second setting of the LTR (LTR_P1, LTR_P2). The EIN's design is now one setting for both routes (`match_ein_lattice`, Xsuite, standard fidelity, every quadrupole keeping its polarity, beta below 1.2 times its design peak in each stretch), in three steps:

1. `p2`: the ETL brings the BAR's beam (stored, so on the BAR's periodic optics where it leaves) to P2 with the optics the beam has there past the BAR, all six (beta, alpha in both planes, dispersion and its slope), with the ETL's six quadrupoles and the LTR's four between its last bend and P1 (`ltr_qua07` to `ltr_qua10`), which set the optics at P1 and, across the bypass, at P2 for both routes. Bmag 2.30 to 1; changes of at most 3 %.
2. `itl`: the ITL's six quadrupoles bring the beam from P1 to the BAR's periodic beta and alpha where it lands. Bmag 8.1 to 1 (with the new P1), changes up to 15 % (`iq6`).
3. `rcs`: the LTR's nine quadrupoles after the merge bring the beam to the RCS's periodic optics where it lands (beta 21.38 m and 6.23 m, alpha 2.62 and -1.09, no dispersion).

The beam then has the same optics from P2 on through the BAR as past it (to every digit printed: beta 46.756 m and 73.354 m at `ltr_qua11` on both routes), and in the RCS the largest beta is its periodic 32.1 m and 30.9 m on both, against 184.6 m and 71.4 m with the design files' strengths.

**The ITL cannot match the dispersion as well, and no setting of the quadrupoles can.** Between the ITL's bends (D1, 0.122 rad, after P1; the septum, -0.12 rad, at the BAR) the quadrupoles keep the dispersion invariant H = γη² + 2αηη' + βη'², and the septum undoes D1's dispersion only for a beam with the BAR's beta at D1, about 16 m; the ETL, the ITL's mirror (the BAR's optics are mirror-symmetric about its septum: the same beta and dispersion at extraction, alpha reversed), needs the same at D2, before P2. The beam past the BAR crosses about 22.8 m of drift between the middles of D1 and D2 (the bypass is 21.8 m), and a beam whose beta is the same at both ends of a drift of length D has at least D there. The designers' two settings of the LTR got round this; with one, the error goes where the stored beam damps it, at BAR injection: dispersion +187 mm, its slope +88 mrad, H = 0.125 m (the emittance grows by ½Hσ_δ² before damping, 62 nm at σ_δ = 10⁻³). Fits that asked for the dispersion as well failed, as this predicts: alone, the ITL and ETL missed beta_x by 190 % and 60 % from 60 random starts each; jointly with the LTR's four quadrupoles, by 27 % and 29 %; with the beta and alpha held, H stayed at 0.124 m whatever the ITL did. Quadrupoles in the bypass, so that the beam past the BAR can have beta near 16 m at P1 and P2, would let both lines match everything.

Tests: a reachable target on the demo injection line (three targets with three quadrupoles); a route's optics leaving a stored ring on its periodic optics (to 1e-8); and, on a fresh import of the EIN, `--match` changes only quadrupole lines of `ltr.yaml`, `itl.yaml` and `etl.yaml`, after which the two routes have the same optics at the merge, neither beats in the RCS, and matching again changes nothing.

## Findings

- **A gradient in a strong bend.** TrackPad first kicked with a bend's gradient as in a straight frame. In the BAR's 45° bends (rho = 1.9 m, with Kn1) that missed 1e-3 of the deflection and moved the BAR's chromaticity by about a unit; more integrator steps did not help (the term was missing, not under-resolved). TrackPad now uses the curved-frame (Maxwellian) field in bends (`CURVED_MULTIPOLES`, 2026-10-08), and the adapter refuses an older TrackPad. Chromaticity of the BAR: MAD-X (-0.0612, -0.0843), TrackPad (-0.0613, -0.0848), Xsuite (-0.0557, +0.0794). TrackPad and Xsuite now agree on the bend body to 1e-6 of its deflection, so Xsuite's vertical value comes from its edge model (it moves by 0.12 with the fringe integrals).
- **The RCS's chromaticity.** MAD-X (0.79, 1.09), Xsuite (0.99, 1.00), TrackPad (0.99, 1.00): the engines agree with each other but not with MAD-X. Not yet understood; the RCS's bends have no gradient.
- **RF voltage and charge.** MAD-X and Xsuite give an energy gain `|q| V sin(phase)`, which accelerates the reference particle whatever its charge. TrackPad's cavity multiplies by the signed charge, so its MAD-X and PALS readers decelerate electrons where MAD-X accelerates them. The adapter passes `|q|`.
- **Patch length.** The lattice's patches have zero length, so a `z_offset` delays the reference particle (as in Xsuite). TrackPad's `Translation` moves the reference time along; the adapter adds the delay back.
- **Expanded bend bodies.** Xsuite's expanded bend body and TrackPad's AT-style bend miss the curvature terms that carry half the chromaticity of a ring with rho ~ 5 m. Neither is used above the fast fidelity.

## Not yet

- Effects the engines have but the adapters do not convert yet: space charge, wakes, beam-beam (both), IBS and spin (Xsuite), radiation in TrackPad (it computes radiation integrals but does not track radiation). Apertures in both.
- The linear fidelity (response matrices from Xsuite's R-matrices or TrackPad's TPSA maps), 6D optics in TrackPad, and native time-dependent cycles (TrackPad's time-dependent parameters, Xsuite's time-dependent knobs) instead of snapshots.
- Settings, tracking and the closed orbit through the worker, for the virtual accelerator (ADR 0003); pinning TrackPad to a release instead of `main`.
- Constraints inside a stretch other than a beta limit (a waist, a phase advance), weights between targets, and matching in TrackPad with its exact TPSA derivatives instead of finite differences.
- Element kinds no adapter converts yet: crab cavities, beam-beam, reference changes; bend pole-face curvature; body shifts along or about the transverse axes.
