# ADR 0007: BPM data, real or virtual, and rings and routes that keep tracking

Status: proposed (2026-10-08). Scope: `eicvibe.diagnostics` (BPM data, file formats, analysis) and `eicvibe.virtual` (rings tracked turn after turn, routes shot after shot, their BPM history). Depends on ADR 0003 (names, modes, the virtual accelerator), ADR 0005 (routes) and ADR 0006 (engines, workers).

## Decisions

1. **One data model for BPM data, whatever produced it.** `TurnByTurn` holds, per plane, every BPM's reading on every turn, `position[bpm, turn]` in mm, with the BPMs' names and s, the sum signal and a status per reading; `OrbitSeries` holds the slow orbit, readings averaged sample after sample. Planes have their own BPM lists, because single-plane BPMs exist (RHIC's do). Analysis (`eicvibe.diagnostics.analysis`: tunes, closed orbit, amplitudes) takes only these, so code written against a real acquisition runs unchanged on a virtual ring's, and back.
2. **Real data is read as it is written; EICViBE writes HDF5.** `read_sdds` reads turn-by-turn SDDS files (SDDS: the self-describing data set format of Argonne's APS; time, fill, trigger; BPM names, gains and averaging per plane; one row per reading, turn after turn, with s, position in mm, the raw A and B signals and a status). Positions are kept as read: what a system's status bits mean is the analysis's decision. HDF5 is the default wherever EICViBE writes BPM data (since 2026-10-21): `write_hdf5` writes an acquisition in EICViBE's own layout (per plane the BPMs' names, s, readings, sum signal and status; the ring, first turn, time, revolution period, source and metadata as attributes), which `read_hdf5` reads back whole; `read_hdf5` also reads HDF5 files of plain matrices (`x0`/`y0`, `[bpm, turn]` in nm, no BPM names). `write_sdds` writes any acquisition in the SDDS layout, so tools made for those files read a virtual ring's data too. The EIC BPM system's own format joins them when it exists.
3. **A virtual ring is tracked as a bunch, turn after turn, in an engine worker.** `RingRun` keeps a bunch of macroparticles and asks the engine for the next turns. The turns of one engine call (a chunk) are not a block of the machine: settings move turn by turn within them (point 6), and their number only paces the run, sized to about a quarter second of tracking from the engine's measured pace (the first call tracks 64 turns), at most `chunk` (1024 by default). The engine returns only what BPMs see: the centroid and the number of particles at every BPM on every turn, and the final coordinates to go on from (`Session.track_beam`, ADR 0006). The run keeps the coordinates, so a worker that dies is replaced and the beam goes on.
4. **A bunch is matched to the optics where it is injected.** In a ring that is the periodic solution: `ring_matching` takes the ring's one-turn map at its start about the closed orbit, by tracking 12 particles one turn (central differences of 1e-8), so it is the map the run tracks (the same engine, fidelity, settings and effects), and builds the bunch from its normal modes: the beam's emittances in the transverse modes, coupling included. With RF on its stable side the map is 6D and the momentum spread sets the longitudinal emittance, so the bunch length is the matched one (the demo ring's 126 mm for 5e-4, the BAR's 51 mm, the RCS's 5 mm); without RF it is 4D, the bunch follows the off-momentum closed orbit through the dispersion, and its length is the beam's own. RF that holds no bunch (point 9) gets the 4D matching of the map with RF off. The bunch is centred: a quiet bunch's centroid is on the closed orbit. A single pass has no periodic solution: its bunch is matched to the optics where its route starts (`route_start`), its first line's initial conditions (`TwissP`, and the orbit of `ParticleP`) or else the machine's optics there, the same optics the viewer's route plot starts from. `run.json` records what the bunch was matched to, and every injection event its bunch length and tunes.
5. **Virtual BPMs read like real ones.** Reading = gain × roll(centroid − offset) + noise, in mm, with each BPM's offset, roll and gain error drawn once and kept hidden (`hidden.json`, not part of the data), and a turn-by-turn resolution whose noise grows as the beam thins; the closed-orbit acquisition has a noise of its own (ADR 0011, with the machine's magnet and supply errors). No particle left means no reading: NaN and status `NO_BEAM`, as real BPMs give nothing without beam. Names are the registry's (EIC names such as `bar-b2`), as the `:rdx`/`:rdy` channels of ADR 0003 are.
6. **Settings change turn by turn, smoothly; the machine logs every command.** A setting change on a ring ramps from the setting's value at that turn to the new one over `ramp_turns` turns (1024 by default; 0 steps), a half cosine (flat at both ends), turn by turn in the engine (`Session.track_beam` with `Ramp`s, ADR 0006), as a magnet follows its supply instead of jumping: the closed orbit moves under the beam, which follows it adiabatically. A change of a setting still ramping takes over from where it is, so nothing jumps. Kicks (a pinger, at the ring's start) and injections come before the next engine call. A ring with a ramp profile ramps on command (`RingRun.ramp()`, amended 2026-10-16, ADR 0013): its reference and RF follow the profile turn by turn to extraction, then it is at injection again with a fresh bunch. Every command, every loss and every error is an event with its turn (a settings event with its ramp's length), and an acquisition carries the events inside it. EICViBE's first version learned this on its energy ramps: settings held for blocks of turns modulate the machine at the block's frequency (a fixed 5-turn block against Q_x = 28.20 grew the horizontal emittance 2.7 times over 2000 turns) and slip the bunch (σ_δ tripled by 30 ms), so its ramp mode moved to a per-turn loop in TrackPad's bridge; there a live change was still a step between batches, and Xsuite kept the blocks (43 ms a turn when ramped). Here both engines ramp every turn, in the engine's own loop, and every change does. Since 2026-10-25 one module makes every turn's settings (`eicvibe.virtual.schedule`, ADR 0013 decision 4): its changes (this half cosine, taken over mid-ramp), a program such as a synchrotron's ramp, which owns its settings while it runs (a change of one applies when it ends), and the supplies' noise (ADR 0011) give the engine's `Ramp`s and reference for each call; `RingRun` asks it, and it is tested without an engine.
7. **A ring's BPMs keep their data as a real BPM system does: a buffer, captures and a slow orbit.** Real systems keep turn-by-turn data only in a circular buffer, frozen and read out on a trigger, and what runs and is archived continuously is an averaged slow orbit: NSLS-II writes turn-by-turn data "continuously into circular memory", stopped by a timing event, with slow acquisition at 10 Hz; RHIC streams up to 1024 turns per beam-synchronous trigger and archives a closed orbit averaged over 10 k turns every 2 s; the LHC captures turns when the user chooses, keeps a post-mortem buffer of the 1000 turns before an abort, and averages its orbit at 10 Hz (Hu et al., PAC 2011 MOP198; Satogata et al., EPAC 2002 THPRI102; LHC BPM acquisition modes). A virtual ring does the same:
   - **The buffer**: every BPM's readings on the latest `buffer_turns` turns (4096 by default; whole chunks, so a few hundred more), overwritten as the beam goes round. The Orbit tab's live view and `turn_by_turn()` read it.
   - **Captures**: the buffer frozen around a trigger and saved, `buffer_turns` long: a kick (an eighth before it, as a pinger's timing event triggers the BPMs), an injection (likewise), the beam lost (a post-mortem: three quarters before the turn it went) and a request (`acquire()`, the admin's or a user's Acquire: the buffer as it is). The buffer keeps the turns a capture waits for. A capture the run ends before is saved with what it has, marked partial. `history.captures()` lists them, `history.capture(id)` reads one as a `TurnByTurn`, `eicvibe-virtual export RUN FILE --capture ID` writes one to a file (HDF5; SDDS for a `.sdds` file); past a size limit (1 GiB) the oldest go.
   - **The slow orbit**: every BPM's mean over each `slow_period` seconds of the run (1 s by default) by the wall clock, the chunk's turns spread over the time they took; kept for good. A virtual second averages the turns the engine tracked in it, about 800 in the BAR with TrackPad, against the millions of a real one; so since 2026-10-13 it averages the readings without their turn-by-turn noise and adds the closed-orbit acquisition's own noise to each sample (ADR 0011), rather than a noise that depended on how fast the simulation ran.
   A run's folder holds `run.json` (what ran, its buffer and slow period), `events.jsonl` (with a `capture` event per capture), `tbt/<first>-<n>.h5` (the buffer's chunks, HDF5), `orbit.bin` (the slow orbit, records appended while readers read it), `captures/<id>.h5` and `.json`, `hidden.json` and `state.json`, each written whole (a temporary file renamed), so readers in other processes never see part of one. Time on the data is the virtual machine's own clock: the run's start plus turns times the revolution period. A route's run records every shot, as transfer lines record every trajectory (the EIC's at 1 Hz).
8. **A route is tracked shot after shot.** A transfer line, or a route through several branches (`routes.yaml`, ADR 0005), is passed once by each bunch, so its BPMs read trajectories, one per shot. `RouteRun` injects a bunch matched to the optics where the route starts (point 4), its centroid moved by shot-to-shot jitter (50 µm and 5 µrad in each plane and 1e-4 in momentum, rms, by default), and tracks it segment by segment in one engine worker with a session per branch (`track_beam` from a segment's first occurrence to its last). Coordinates cross every join as they are: the offset where an injection lands is the injected beam's trajectory, which the BPMs see (a route's optics, which start every segment on its design orbit, leave it out). Each shot is one "turn" of the run's history, whose unit is `shot`: the same files, readers and API as a ring's, with `kind` `route`, the route's name as its branch and `route:<name>` as its branch id. Settings change between shots. A route whose reference momentum changes at a join is refused, except where it leaves a ring the beam is accelerated in (`accelerated`, ADR 0005): there every particle gains the same momentum, so its angles and momentum deviation shrink by p0/p1 (adiabatic damping; the RCS to the RTE, 0.75 to 10 GeV/c) and its position stays. A route that goes round a ring (`turns`) reads its BPMs on every pass, each pass a reading of its own along the route, with the one BPM's hidden offset and gain.
9. **A configuration that cannot hold a beam is simulated, and said.** With the longitudinal effect the run checks every cavity's phase against the transition (stable where the slip factor times cos(phase) is negative) and records a warning, then tracks what is configured, as the real machine would.

10. **A ring run's tracking is checked against its model, as a real ring's optics are measured.** `/api/v1/runs/{run}/check` (the viewer's Check tab, and `check.html`) analyses a stretch of turn-by-turn readings, by default the latest kick's capture from the kick on: the tune from the spectrum (Hann window, interpolated peak), oriented and given its integer part by the phase advance along the ring; each BPM's phase and amplitude at the tune; the phase advance and beta (from the amplitudes, BPM gain errors included) against the model; the closed orbit (the mean readings, BPM offsets included) against the model's; and the tune and amplitude over the turns, in stretches, as the bunch decoheres. The model is the engine's optics and closed orbit of the run's lattice with the settings it had at the start of the stretch: those it started with (`run.json` `values`, since 2026-10-10) and its settings events. On request the same engine tracks eight single particles kicked up to the measured amplitude, for the tune each amplitude should have: a large kick moves the tune, and the curve tells a correct tracking from a wrong one. The analysis functions (`spectrum`, `harmonics`, `phase_errors`, `beta_beating`, `tune_drift` in `eicvibe.diagnostics`) take only BPM data, so they work on a real ring's acquisitions too.

A ring run's **tune monitor** reads the betatron tunes without a kick, as a BBQ does: a bunch of finitely many particles moves as a whole a little, and the power spectra of its centroid at every BPM over the last 1024 turns, summed, peak at the tunes (lines below 1.5 synchrotron tunes are ignored). The reading (fractional tunes folded into 0 to 0.5, the turn, whether a setting was ramping in those turns) is `RingRun.tunes` and `GET /va/machines/{m}/tunes`. On the RCS it reads (0.20017, 0.43756) against the model's (0.2002, 0.4375); on the demo ring the vertical reads 0.3020 where the tune is 0.3139: in that ring's weak vertical signal a synchrotron sideband (Qy + Qs, folded) is the strongest line, as a passive monitor can lock on one. A sideband moves with the tune, so a tune change is verified as the monitor's change (ADR 0010).

## Using it

```python
from eicvibe.virtual import RingRun, Beam, BpmHistory
from eicvibe.diagnostics import tune, read_hdf5, read_sdds, write_hdf5

run = RingRun(lattice, "bar", "runs/bar1", registry=registry, values={(cavity, "RFP.phase"): pi}).start()
run.kick(x=2e-4)                  # before the next engine call; logged as an event
run.update({(corrector, "MagneticMultipoleP.Kn0L"): 1e-5})  # ramps in over 1024 turns; logged
tbt = run.history.turn_by_turn()  # the latest 1024 turns; any notebook can open runs/bar1 instead
tune(tbt, "x"), tune(read_sdds("tbt.Wed_Sep__4_19:08:18_2024.sdds"), "x")   # the same analysis
write_hdf5(tbt, "bar1.h5"); tune(read_hdf5("bar1.h5"), "x")                   # to a file and back
```

`eicvibe-virtual ring MACHINE --branch bar --out runs/bar1` runs one from the command line (`--ramp-turns` sets how long a change takes), `eicvibe-virtual show runs/bar1` reads it, and `eicvibe-virtual export runs/bar1 tbt.h5` writes turns as HDF5 (`tbt.sdds`, or `--format sdds`, in the SDDS layout). A route runs the same way:

```python
from eicvibe.virtual import RouteRun, Jitter

run = RouteRun(machine.lattice, route, "runs/direct", registry=machine.registry, rate=2.0).start()
run.update({(corrector, "MagneticMultipoleP.Kn0L"): 1e-5})  # between shots; logged
shots = run.history.turn_by_turn()                         # [bpm, shot] trajectories
```

or `eicvibe-virtual route MACHINE --route direct --out runs/direct` (`--through ltr,rcs` for a route the machine does not name). `--mode MODE` starts either from an operating mode's settings (`settings/MODE.yaml`), and `--set` changes single values after it; the EIN's two routes need none, as one setting serves both (ADR 0006). Both print what the bunch is matched to; `eicvibe-web MACHINE --runs runs` shows every run under `runs/` in the Orbit tab, including runs started after the server.

## Validation (2026-10-08)

- The SDDS acquisition of 2024-09-04 (the Blue ring) reads as 168 horizontal and 167 vertical BPMs over 1024 turns, and writes back identically. Its horizontal tune reads 0.3005 (the ring's .695 seen from one BPM as 1 − q). An HDF5 file of matrices of 2019-10-24 gives 0.2185 and 0.2603, its ring's tunes.
- A one-particle bunch's centroid equals single-particle tracking in both engines to round-off; TrackPad's and Xsuite's centroids of a 30-particle bunch over 128 turns agree to 1e-8 m.
- The BAR, kicked: tunes from its BPM data 0.42139, 0.47456; from its optics 0.42141, 0.47458. Quiet, the BPMs read their offsets and their 5 µm noise.
- Speed, 50 particles in the BAR: about 760 turns per second with TrackPad (after its 18 s start), 200 particles over 1024 turns in 4.7 s. Xsuite took 34 s for the same at the standard fidelity, nearly all in the BAR's combined-function bends, where its adaptive integrator takes many kicks; its fast fidelity now integrates bends in ten Yoshida kicks (13 µs per particle and turn, tunes within 1e-3).

- The demo's `transfer` route (injection line, part of the ring, extraction line): a corrector in the ring changes the trajectory downstream as linear optics predict (`-θ √(β β_k) sin Δμ`, within 2 % of its largest value) and leaves it upstream unchanged; a beam lost in the ring reads `NO_BEAM` downstream and the line before still reads it. 12 shots of 20 particles along the EIN's BAR route (210 BPMs) take 1.3 s with TrackPad.

- Matching, on the demo ring with both engines: without RF, beta, alpha and dispersion from the one-turn map equal the engines' own optics (to 1e-6); with RF, the map's normal modes turn in their own planes (W⁻¹MW block-diagonal to 1e-15), the matched bunch keeps its length within 1.3 % over 120 turns (the RF's curvature), and the same bunch three times longer breathes between 137 and 378 mm.

- Settings ramps (2026-10-09): one centroid on the demo ring's closed orbit, a corrector changed by 5e-5 rad (the orbit moves 0.24 mm): after a step it rings about the new orbit by 0.24 mm rms; after a ramp of 64 turns by 0.009 mm, 256 turns 0.0005 mm, 1024 turns 0.00002 mm; changed again in the middle of a 256-turn ramp, 0.0017 mm (two steps: 0.58 mm). Cost in the BAR, 50 particles: TrackPad 853 turns per second without a ramp, 854 ramping a corrector, 841 ramping all ten quadrupoles (its first ramped call compiles for 0.4 s); Xsuite (fast) 1480, 1445 and 1260 to 1340.

- The check (2026-10-10). The demo ring kicked by 0.15 mrad: tunes as the model's to 1e-4, phase advance to 0.02° rms, beta from the amplitudes within 1.7 % rms (the BPMs' 1 % gain errors). The EIN's RCS kicked by 1 mrad (Xsuite, standard fidelity, 200 particles): the measured tune is 0.2525 against the model's 28.2002, because the kick gives 33 mm of amplitude (at beta 32 m), where the sextupoles detune the beam; single particles give 0.2002 at 0.3 mm, 0.2050 at 9.7 mm and 0.2544 at 33 mm, both engines alike, so the tracking is right. After that kick the bunch decoheres to 1.2 mm of centroid motion, 16.4 mm over the square root of its 200 particles, which goes on, since nothing damps it: a few-sigma kick (20 to 100 µrad in the RCS) measures the linear optics.

- The buffer, captures and slow orbit (2026-10-09): on the demo ring, a kick's capture holds its 512 turns with 64 before the kick and gives the ring's horizontal tune; a request captures the buffer as it was; a kick of 0.5 rad loses the beam and the post-mortem capture holds 384 turns before the loss and none after; turn by turn only the buffer remains. From the command line, a 6000-turn demo run kept a 4096-turn buffer, five 1 s samples of slow orbit and three captures (injection, two kicks, the last partial), and a capture exported in the SDDS layout.

- HDF5 as the default (2026-10-21): an acquisition written by `write_hdf5` reads back identically (BPM names, s, readings with their NaNs, sum signal, status, time, revolution period, events); a run's chunks and captures are HDF5 files (about 3 kB of header each, against a chunk's megabyte); `eicvibe-virtual export` writes HDF5 unless the file ends in `.sdds` or `--format sdds` is given. The slow orbit stays `orbit.bin`: it is appended to while other processes read it, which HDF5 allows only in its single-writer mode (SWMR), with the writer holding the file open for the whole run.

## Findings

- **The BAR's RF phase.** `BAR.madx` gives its cavity `LAG=0`, the unstable phase for electrons above the transition (eta = 0.079). MAD-X's optics do not notice; a stored beam does: its energy runs away and the dispersion carries the orbit with it (0.7 mm without any kick). A stored beam needs LAG 0.5 (`RFP.phase` = π); the BAR mode settings should set it.

- **The EIN's injection into the RCS.** The LTR ends with a patch (`ltr_pat01`) that puts the injected beam at x = −30.9 mm in the RCS, and the RCS's injection kicker (`mqa1_il_ik`, `rcs-t10`, Bmad's `ltr_rcs_kicker`, HKICK = −2.945 mrad) is meant to put it on the axis. In Bmad the patch's `x_pitch` is −4.391 mrad, which gives the beam px = +4.391 mrad: it reaches the kicker 0.39 mm from the axis with px = +2.930 mrad, and the kick takes that out. The staged MAD-X had the angle's sign flipped, by Bmad's own MAD-X writer (`YROTATION = -x_pitch`; ADR 0002), so the imported LTR sent the beam 150 mm rms off in the RCS, and the shots of both routes into it were lost. The same writer had made the LTR's 15 BPMs drifts and named its magnets after the pipes they sit on. With the conversion corrected (2026-10-10), the LTR has its BPMs and names, and both routes take the beam round the RCS: 47 mm off axis with the injection kicker at 0, within 4 mm with it at −2.945 mrad (Kn0L = +2.945e-3). No mode sets the kicker yet. The RTE's extraction patch had the same flipped sign; the beam now meets it on the RCS's axis, 15 mm and 3.1 mrad from the RTE's, because the RCS's extraction kickers (`es.pip01_ek1003`, `ek1004`, `es.pip02_ek1005`; about −0.86 mrad each in the RTE design) are at 0 too.

## Not yet

- A shot along a route that stores its beam in a ring (`stored`, the EIN's BAR) passes the ring once, as a line; the route's optics (ADR 0004) and a match (ADR 0006) have the stored beam leave on the ring's periodic optics. Storing it would mean tracking the ring's damping (thousands of turns) or replacing the bunch by the ring's equilibrium bunch where it is extracted.

- Publishing the readings as pvAccess channels (`<bpm>:rdx`/`:rdy` from the slow orbit, turn-by-turn waveforms on a trigger) and serving the history to the REPLAY service (ADR 0003). The viewer shows a run's orbits every N turns (ADR 0004), from the run's folder; `state.json` tells it whether the run goes on.
- A fast acquisition stream (10 kHz at NSLS-II) for orbit feedback, and kicks, injections and captures at a set turn rather than at the next engine call (a quarter second at most).
- A setting's ramp from its supply's own slew rate (the registry's), instead of one length for the machine; a route's settings change between shots, as a line's magnets settle between pulses.
- Injection settings (the RCS injection kicker on for the first turn), and handing a route's beam on to a ring run.
- Radiation damping in a run (Xsuite has it; the run passes the effect through, untested), several bunches, a pinger at its own place in the ring, BPM nonlinearity, pacing a run to the machine's clock.
- Binary SDDS, and the EIC BPM system's own format.
