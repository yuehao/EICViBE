# ADR 0013: A synchrotron's ramp: profiles in the machine files, a moving reference in the engines, a ramp on command

Status: accepted (2026-10-16). Scope: ramp profiles (`eicvibe.domain.machine.ramps`, `eicvibe.store.ramps`, the machine folder's `ramps/`, amending ADR 0005), their import from the RCS parameter database (`eicvibe.importers.rcs_database`, `eicvibe.machines.ein`), the engines' tracking with a moving reference (amends ADR 0006), and the virtual ring's ramp (amends ADR 0007) with its command in the service (amends ADR 0008).

## Context

The EIN's RCS is a rapid-cycling synchrotron: it takes the beam at 750 MeV and ramps it to 9 GeV in 108 ms, once a second. The RCS parameter database (`einlattices/03_RCS Parameter Database.xlsx`, V. Ranjbar) gives the ramp of the 40-cell ring in two sheets. "9GeV Lat" defines the energy as a waveform in turns: a 10 ms injection porch; a smooth start, quartic in the turn, over 4000 turns up to the largest ramp rate, 86.25 GeV/s; a linear ramp at that rate through the intrinsic spin resonances up to 8.813 GeV; a smooth end, quartic again, into 9 GeV; a 10 ms flattop; then the ramp down, a dwell, the return to injection and 744 ms of settling, which make the cycle 1 s. It also tabulates the waveform every millisecond with the dipole field and current (0.054 to 0.649 T; 287 to 3446 A with 6 turns and a 4 cm gap). "RF Ramp 9GeV Lat" gives the RF the designers computed for it: the two cavities' total voltage, from 1.698 MV (the value the virtual RCS has been pinned at, ADR 0007) to 15.9 MV, and the synchronous phase from the zero crossing, from 0 to 52°, every 0.75 ms of the ramp, with the radiation loss in it (12.45 MeV a turn at 9 GeV). Until now the virtual RCS stayed at injection.

The legacy EICViBE simulated this ramp (`docs/design/ramp_mode.md` there, read-only). What it learned: the reference must move inside the engine, turn by turn; a reference held fixed for a block of turns and stepped between blocks both modulates the machine at the block period (ε_x grew 2.7 times) and makes a phase-slip excursion that grows σ_δ; the RF's sign convention must be checked, not assumed; and synchrotron radiation is not optional in the upper half of the ramp, where the damping time is a fifteenth of the ramp and the RF program is built around the loss.

The engines differ there. Xsuite tracks radiation (mean energy loss and damping, and quantum excitation) and moves a bunch's reference with `update_p0c_and_energy_deviations`, as its own energy programs do. TrackPad (0.2.0) radiates only in wigglers: its bends and quadrupoles carry a `rad` flag that tracking does not read, although its optics give the radiation integrals.

## Decisions

1. **A ring's ramp is a machine file: `ramps/<name>.yaml` and its RF table, `ramps/<name>.csv`** (the format ADR 0005 reserved `ramps/` for, defined now from real data). The YAML holds the ring (`branch`), the energy program as its designers' waveform and parameters (`injection`, `extraction`, `linear_end` in eV of total energy, `rate` in eV/s, `smooth_turns`), the injection porch, the flattop and the cycle in seconds, the cavities that share the RF (all of the ring's when none are listed), and its source with its SHA-256. The CSV holds the RF program: time from the ramp's start (s), voltage (V, all cavities), synchronous phase (rad, from the zero crossing, positive where the bunch gains energy) and the designers' energy at that time. The energy program is kept as parameters, not samples: it is evaluated turn by turn exactly as the designers' sheet does (`RampProfile`, `EnergyRamp`); the RF program is their table, interpolated linearly and held at its ends. A turn is the revolution period at the speed of light, as in their sheet. `eicvibe check` checks a ramp against its ring (`ramp_problems`): a ring, its cavities, its reference at the injection energy, an RF program that covers the ramp and was computed for its energies (to 1e-3).
2. **The RCS's ramp comes from the parameter database** (`read_rcs_ramp`). The importer finds the parameters by the sheet's own labels (`E0=`, `Ef=`, `EendLR`, `RE=`, `N1=`, `t1=` to `t7=`, `Circ`), rebuilds the waveform and requires it to equal the sheet's millisecond table (it does, to 1e-15) and its ramp to last the sheet's `t2` (107.873 ms). `python -m eicvibe.machines.ein FOLDER --ramp` adds it to a machine folder, changing no other file; a new folder gets it with the rest.
3. **The engines track a moving reference** (amends ADR 0006). `track_beam(..., momentum=pc)` takes the reference momentum during each turn of the call. Magnets keep their normalized strengths, as a synchrotron's magnets track its energy; before each turn the bunch is put about that turn's reference (px, py and delta rescaled, zeta / beta0 kept). The coordinates are about the first turn's reference and the result about the last's. A ramp tracked call after call puts the bunch about the next call's first reference in between (`eicvibe.engines.adapter.rereferenced`), as the engines do between turns (corrected 2026-10-18, below). It needs the RF to act (effect longitudinal) and a ring's turns, and is the native `cycle` time scale of both engines. Xsuite rescales its particles as its energy programs do; TrackPad's adapter does it in its Julia loop and rebuilds the RF cavities every turn the reference moves (TrackPad normalizes a cavity's kick by the energy it was built with). Settings that ramp at the same time (the RF program) are the existing per-turn `Ramp`s. The conformance suite holds every engine to it: a ring whose reference rises as its cavity gives the energy keeps the bunch on the reference and damps the betatron action as 1/p (within 2e-4), and a reference that does not move tracks as without one.
4. **The virtual ring ramps on command** (the user's choice; amends ADR 0007 and 0008). A ring with a ramp profile holds its beam at injection, as before, until an operator ramps it (`RingRun.ramp()`; `POST /machines/{m}/beam` with `action: ramp`; the admin page's Ramp tab (decision 6) and the Ramp button in the viewer's machine menu; `eicvibe-virtual ring --ramp-at TURN`). Then, turn by turn, the reference follows the energy program and the cavities the RF program (the voltage shared equally, the synchronous phase on the stable side of the zero crossing: pi - phi_s above transition); engine calls end at extraction. At extraction, after the flattop, the bunch leaves (an `extract` event with its energy, particles, emittances, momentum spread and length) and the ring is at injection again: its ramp down, which has no beam, is not tracked and takes no turns; its RF settings are back and a fresh bunch is injected. The BPMs read every turn of a ramp, as of any turn, and the readings of the whole of it, from its first turn to extraction, are kept as a capture `ramp` (2026-10-20): a ramp is longer than the BPMs' buffer, which keeps its turns until the capture is saved at extraction (a run stopped before saves what it has); 58.5 MB for the RCS's 23 450 turns and 192 BPMs, the run's captures kept up to its retention (1 GB by default, the oldest dropped first). The admin page's Ramp tab names each ramp's capture, and the viewer's BPM view reads it as any capture. The events `ramp`, `flattop`, `extract`, `porch` and `inject` mark it; a machine's `cycle` says where it is (injection, ramping or flattop, and the energy), the run's info the profile. While it ramps the RF program sets the cavities: RF settings written then apply at injection (a warning event says so); magnet settings and kicks act as always; a ramping ring takes no new bunch.
5. **Ramps run on Xsuite, with radiation; TrackPad leaves them** (the user's choice). A ring with a ramp profile tracks with Xsuite by default, with mean radiation and quantum excitation; a run without radiation damping refuses to ramp. TrackPad still tracks a moving reference (decision 3), for ramps without radiation and to check Xsuite. *Amended 2026-10-19:* TrackPad radiates since 607dc13 (below), so a ring ramps on either engine, each with its mean radiation and quantum excitation; Xsuite stays the default, four times as fast on the RCS. Xsuite's quantum excitation is its `quantum-kick` model since the same day (ADR 0015). A bunch's matching takes the normal modes of the one-turn map without radiation, about the closed orbit found with it (where the energy loss puts the bunch): radiation damps the map a little, and normal modes are a symplectic map's.
6. **The admin page has a Ramp tab** (amends ADR 0008), shown when a machine ramps, opened from the tab or a machine's Ramp… button. For the chosen ring it shows where the ring is (at injection, ramping or flattop; its energy; the turn of the ramp against extraction, with a progress bar), the control that starts a ramp with a reason for the log (for an operator on a shared machine, the owner on a sandbox; disabled with the reason when the machine refuses: stopped, ramping, or on an engine without radiation), the profile's three programs against time from the ramp's start (energy, the cavities' voltage, the synchronous phase; one chart each, with the flattop and the ring's turn now), the profile's parameters and source, and the ramps of the machine's run with the bunch each extracted (particles extracted of those injected, energy, emittances, momentum spread, length). `GET /machines/{m}/ramp` gives it all (`RampView`): the programs sampled at 241 times, the cycle, the refusal, and the last 20 ramps from the run's events.

## Not done yet

- The supplies' currents during the ramp: magnets keep their normalized strengths, so their currents follow the energy through the design transfer functions (ADR 0014); the supplies' voltage, which limits how fast a current changes, is not modelled.
- What the real ramp has beyond the programs: tracking errors between magnet families, eddy currents at the start, and the RF's beam loops (radial and phase); the virtual beam follows the programs open loop.
- The extracted bunch goes nowhere: extraction into the RTE, which is the 10 GeV design while this ramp ends at 9 GeV.
- Cycling at 1 Hz, and the ramp down: the sheet's linear ramp down takes as long as the ramp up at the full rate, so it would go below zero energy; it has no beam and is not used here.
- Speed: one thread, about 110 turns a second with 20 particles; Xsuite's OpenMP contexts are not used.

## Upstream finding (TrackPad 0.2.0), resolved in 607dc13 (2026-10-19)

TrackPad does not radiate in bends and quadrupoles: only wigglers emit (`_wig_radiation_kicks`), and the `rad` flag that `Bend` and `Quadrupole` carry is not read in tracking. Its optics compute the radiation integrals already. For an electron ring above a few GeV this is a physics gap, not a convenience: without the mean energy loss the RCS's RF program over-accelerates by 12.45 MeV a turn at 9 GeV (the bunch would slide about 50° in phase), and without damping and quantum excitation the flattop's emittance and energy spread are wrong. Proposal: the mean energy loss and its damping in bends and quadrupoles behind the existing flag (the wiggler's kick is the template), then quantum excitation.

TrackPad 607dc13 did it: `rad=1` gives the classical loss and damping in bends, thick multipoles and superconducting magnets, `rad=2` adds quantum excitation as Gaussian photon diffusion per integration slice (Sands' variance), its random numbers a hash of the seed, turn, element, particle and slice, so serial and threaded tracking agree; optics ignore `rad`. The adapter builds radiating copies of the magnets for a tracking call, gives every call (and every segment between BPMs, where TrackPad's element count starts again) its own seed, and finds a ring's closed orbit with radiation itself (Newton's method on a radiating turn), as TrackPad's finders ignore `rad`. The conformance suite holds both engines to Sands: one turn of a 3 GeV ring of 10 m bends loses U0 = (4 pi / 3) r_e m c^2 gamma^4 / rho to 2e-3, and quantum excitation spreads delta by sqrt(2 Cq gamma^2 (U0 / E) / rho) to 6 %.

## Correction (2026-10-18)

The virtual ring passed each call's final coordinates, which are about that call's last reference, to the next call as if about its first: every call boundary skipped one turn's step of the reference (4.3e-4 in delta at 1 GeV), a kick at the calls' period. Tracking through an engine host in calls of 16 turns showed it as a horizontal line at 1/16 (ADR 0015). The ring now puts the bunch about the next call's first reference (`rereferenced`: each particle's momentum kept, as the engines do between turns), and the conformance suite holds every engine to a ramp tracked in two calls being the ramp tracked in one (to 1e-9). The RCS figures below dated 2026-10-16 were taken with the fault, at calls of about 27 turns.

## Validation (2026-10-16)

- The waveform from the database's parameters equals its millisecond table to 1e-15 and lasts its 107.8734713797391 ms; the RF table's energies agree with it to 1.6e-6; the RCS of the machine folder passes `ramp_problems` (its circumference, 1506.934 m, is the sheet's).
- Both engines, a ring whose reference rises 1 % in 200 turns as its cavity gives the energy: the bunch stays on the reference (delta 4.9507e-5 after the last cavity, the last turn's gain, to 1e-7) and its action falls as p0/p (0.990183 against 0.990148); the engines agree to 1e-9.
- The demo ring, ramped by command from 2.000 to 2.010 GeV in 250 turns: flattop, extraction 50 turns later with every particle, and a fresh bunch at injection.
- The EIN's RCS (the machine folder's lattice, Xsuite, standard fidelity, radiation and quantum excitation, 20 particles), ramped by command: 21 461 turns to the flattop, extraction 1989 turns later with every particle, at 9 GeV, the bunch on the reference (mean delta -1.6e-4 and 4.5e-5 in two runs). At extraction, ε_x 51 and 56 nm (the database's equilibrium at 9 GeV: 49 nm), ε_y damped to 1e-15, σ_δ 0.80e-3 and 1.46e-3 (their mean 1.13e-3; the database's 1.14e-3), σ_z 19 mm. 210 s of wall clock for the 23 450 turns (800 s before Xsuite kept its monitored line between calls, ADR 0006).
- The admin page's Ramp tab, on the EIN's RCS (a scratch service, Xsuite, 20 particles): the ramp started from the tab; the tab followed it (energy, turn, the now marker on the programs) through the flattop to extraction, every particle extracted at 9 GeV (ε_x 33.7 nm, ε_y 1.3e-15 m, σ_δ 1.01e-3, σ_z 13 mm: the 20 particles' scatter about the runs above), and the ring back at injection with the control enabled.

## TrackPad's speed (2026-10-19)

TrackPad was four times slower than Xsuite on the RCS, for two reasons, both measured. The adapter gave every thick magnet 10 integration steps at the standard fidelity, where the bends (56 % of a turn) and sextupoles (17 %) need far fewer: bends at 2 steps and sextupoles at 1 change the tunes and chromaticity by nothing measurable, while the quadrupoles, integrated by drifts and kicks, need about 10 for tunes to 1e-5 (the error goes as the fourth power of the step). And TrackPad's radiation split every step in two around its kick, so a radiating magnet cost three times a conservative one (Xsuite: 17 % more).

The adapter now chooses steps as Xsuite chooses its kicks (ADR 0006), a pure quadrupole in closed form as TrackPad's `LBend(L, 0.0; K=k1)` (the expanded Hamiltonian, as Xsuite's thick quadrupole), and the optics of a lattice with an `LBend` are TrackPad's finite differences. TrackPad daad9e7 (2026-10-19, merged from a branch written for it) takes the radiation once per step, after it, at the step's mean, as Xsuite does, and lets `LBend` radiate; TrackPad's own suite passes on it (1936 tests), with six new `LBend` tests. The adapter uses closed-form quadrupoles only where `LBend` radiates (an older TrackPad keeps integrated ones); EICViBE's Julia environment has daad9e7.

### On Julia's threads (2026-10-20)

A ramp on one thread fitted about 15 particles in a minute; eight to ten separate processes, each its own bunch, about 110, but nothing combined them. TrackPad's threaded `track!` already spread a bunch over threads, one chunk each and one wait a turn, but read no BPMs on the way, so the adapter cut the ring at its 192 BPMs and tracked the stretches on one thread. TrackPad 8e31071 (2026-10-20, merged from a branch written for it) lets `track!` observe the bunch after chosen elements (the survivors counted, their x and y added up, each thread's chunk on its own, combined after the turn), and makes its threaded loop cheap: a chunk passes through the turn as one object, one dynamic dispatch per element and nothing boxed (8 MB a turn allocated before, 0.2 MB now), and the check for wakes resolves only time-varying elements. The adapter then tracks the ring whole, one `track!` a turn of a ramp, on `auto` threads (12 on the M3 Max).
